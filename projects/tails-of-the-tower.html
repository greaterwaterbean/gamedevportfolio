Overview

Tails of the Tower is a hack-and-slash action game that runs in the browser: climb the tower, beat the Rat King, and save Faunia. Between climbs you're back in the tavern, talking to your allies, choosing a class and spending the gold and orbs you brought down. The 1.0 build has three acts, more than ten enemy types and three classes with their own movesets, and takes about 40 minutes to finish.

It was built by "Too Many Cooks," a 24-student practical capstone studio, across a 15-week semester. The studio started with 24 people pitching ideas in an "atomized" ideation process and narrowed down to one concept everyone would build together. I was one of nine programmers on the resulting team.

My Contributions

  • Programmed enemy and level systems as an assigned programmer on a 9-person programming team.
  • Designed or co-designed every enemy and level in the game, laying out early levels on paper and chalkboard before building them in-engine.
  • Built the game's first enemy, the slime, then prototyped the ranged archer and the Hob Lobber, a hobgoblin that lobs exploding potions at where you're standing. Teammates finished their combat hookup, and both made it into the shipped acts.
  • Added typed keys and locks, a key HUD and doors that seal rooms, and set up the act-by-act level pack the final game's runs are built from.
  • Owned the team's GitHub repository: managed the branching workflow, set up Discord webhooks for commit activity, and reviewed every commit that went into the build.
  • Took part in the studio's 24-person ideation sessions that narrowed the group down to this concept.

Playtest 1: can a stranger pick it up?

March 13, 2026 · beta.2

The first outside playtest was a small slice of the game, just the tavern hub and one combat demo, played online by people we'd never met. We wanted to know if someone with nobody there to explain it could figure the game out. Back then it was still called Serve & Slash.

How we ran it

  • Shipped it as a browser build on itch.io, so testers had nothing to install.
  • Let the game do the teaching, with first-time tutorial pop-ups in the tavern and control prompts in the combat demo.
  • Asked testers one specific question in the release notes: how did the tavern tutorial pop-ups land?
  • Locked the main menu to the tavern and commented out unfinished work so it couldn't break the build. An NPC tells you when you've reached the end of what's built.

My part

  • The slime testers fought in the combat demo. It was the game's first enemy, which I built and teammates tuned.
  • A bestiary that sorted every enemy idea by its core "factors", as groundwork for one base enemy class the rest could build on.
  • Enemy variants that reuse existing sprites, like a slime that splits and grows, so new enemies didn't mean new work for the art team.

What changed next

  • A pause menu and a "thanks for testing" end screen, so every session had a clear ending.
  • Export presets went into the repo, so every web build came out the same way.
  • Classes and the prologue arrived in beta.3.

Playtest 2: only the levels that were ready

April 13, 2026 · beta.4

Our second official playtest added prologue art, branching dialogue, and classes that change your moveset, weapon and special moves. It was also the first build called Tails of the Tower, since the game had stopped being about serving food. More content meant more ways for a session to go wrong, so we were careful about what went in.

How we ran it

  • Only levels that were close to finished went into the playtest folder, and we cut the run from three levels to two to keep sessions short.
  • Every tester started as a new player, with first-time tutorials on and gold at zero, so nobody inherited a developer's save.
  • The night before, we added guards against double-clicked dialogue choices and enemies dying twice, and left the deeper enemy-state bug for a proper fix after the playtest.

My part

  • The Hob Lobber, which lobs potions that explode where you were standing.
  • The ranged archer, firing arrows on a cooldown. Shaina finished its hit detection.
  • A level-jam draft of floor 1 that I posted to the team for reactions, then redesigned around four key rooms. Each one sits behind two battle rooms, and all four keys together open the boss room.
  • Doors that seal you into a room while you fight.

What changed next

  • Dialogue portraits and an art pass across the whole game, in beta.5.
  • A practice room with a training dummy, and a controls card that highlights the right mouse button.
  • A new way of organizing levels (next entry).

Playtest 3: the whole climb

May 4, 2026 · Release 0.9

Playtest 3 was the first build you could play from start to finish, with three acts, bosses, a title screen, credits and a results screen. Levels were now coming from several designers, so the build needed a structure to hold them all.

How we ran it

  • We built a level pack for the test. Each act's run draws levels at random from that act's folder and ends in a boss room, and unfinished levels sit in a Testing folder outside the rotation.
  • Difficulty climbs by act. Faster, tougher rats and a stronger hawk boss show up from Act 2 on, the camera pulls back as levels get more complex, and beating Act 1 unlocks Act 2.
  • We fixed things that only show up on real setups. Q and E became a fallback for Mac trackpads that don't register a scroll wheel, the intro rat dropped from 10 hits to 5, and spawners can't be destroyed before they spawn anything.

My part

  • I set up the act-by-act folders the runs are drawn from, so the game's progression is visible in the project itself.
  • Typed keys and locks, plus a HUD showing which keys you're carrying.
  • Room layouts and locked doors across the starting levels, and a first draft of an Act 3 level.

What changed next

  • A mini map on every level and a minimum camera zoom, so no level starts too tight to read.
  • Stat changes so the Rogue and the Mage hold up early on.
  • Pause and settings menus moved so the mini map stopped covering their buttons.

Ship day: 1.0

May 13, 2026 · Release 1.0

The last two weeks went to a full pass over every system with no new features, and the game went out as 1.0 on May 13.

The final pass

  • Readability passes on the menus and journal, buttons that sit in the same place on every screen, and a fix for a tavern crash.
  • Performance work so the browser build runs on older machines.

How it came together

  • A new build on itch.io every two to three weeks from March on (beta.2 to beta.5, then 0.9 and 1.0), each with notes on what changed.
  • A public repo with a README that points to the latest playtest build, so anyone could play what we were making.

What shipped

  • Three acts, each closed by a boss room, with the Rat King at the top.
  • Three classes, Warrior, Rogue and Mage, each with its own moveset.
  • More than ten enemy types and about 40 minutes of play.
